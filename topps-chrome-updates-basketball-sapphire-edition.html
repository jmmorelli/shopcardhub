How It Works

Direct Drop, No Draw — But No Price Yet

Sapphire editions sell direct on Topps.com rather than through the EQL draw — but they are online exclusives with tight allocations, and the base Updates release already proved demand by selling through its draw. Set your number before checkout opens: this is a first-year product with no prior-year price history, so the drop price itself is the first real data point. If you miss the window, sealed boxes surface on eBay immediately — compare any premium against what the box actually contains before paying it.

Variables That Move This Set

⬛ HIGH IMPACT
The Drop Price
First-year product, no price history. Baseball Sapphire editions have stepped up year over year, and where Topps sets this box decides the entire EV math. Nothing on this page can be priced until checkout opens Sep 3.
◯ MEDIUM
Flagg's Year-Two Arc
Flagg enters year two as reigning ROY and the most liquid name in basketball. His Future Stars Sapphire autos and any Padparadscha price the whole product — his market is deep enough that star pulls stay liquid.
☉ LOWER
Sapphire Fatigue
This is the second basketball Sapphire drop in weeks (Chrome Black, Updates Sapphire) inside a crowded Topps calendar. If collector budgets thin out, non-numbered Sapphire base cools first — numbered colors and autos hold better.
Avoid

What to Skip

✗
Paying drop-week premiums on sealed — online-exclusive scarcity inflates week-one asks; supply and prices normalize once break results circulate. Let the FOMO wave pass.
✗
Non-numbered Sapphire base on non-stars — the tech is pretty, but unnumbered base on role players is volume, not scarcity. The ladder's value lives in numbered colors and autos.
✗
Buying “Sapphire” listings without checking the product line — base Chrome, Chrome Black, and Updates Sapphire listings blur together in search. Confirm the exact product and year on every listing.
