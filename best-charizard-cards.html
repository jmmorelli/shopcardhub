Gengar's Collector's Desk · No. 1 · Charizard

The Charizard cards worth collecting

Charizard is the card that made this hobby. Every era since 1999 has printed one that collectors still chase, and most of the rest is filler with a dragon on it. These are the ones I'd put in the binder, from a $13 Radiant to a six-figure Crystal, with what each one actually sells for.

Shelf one · The grails

The grails

The Charizards that define a collection. Vintage WOTC, one print run each, and nearly all of the value sits in the top grade.

The one

Base Set Charizard #4 (1999)

This is the card. Holo, 120 HP, Fire Spin, the art every other Charizard is measured against. It is really three cards: 1st Edition (the black stamp, first print), Shadowless (no drop shadow on the art box, same early print without the stamp) and Unlimited (the one most of us pulled). Separate PSA labels, separate pop reports, separate prices — a collector picks one lane and knows which lane they are in.

Per pokeinvest's pop read (Aug 20 2026) there were 123 PSA 10 1st Editions and 58 PSA 10 Shadowless, so the Shadowless 10 is the rarer slab even though the 1st Edition carries the bigger price.

Last WOTC card

Crystal Charizard, Skyridge #146 (2003)

Skyridge was the final set Wizards of the Coast made before Nintendo took the game back, and the Crystal Charizard is its secret rare: a holo with the Crystal Type mechanic on a card that most kids never saw in a pack. It trades almost only graded, so there is no clean raw price — which is itself the tell that this is a collector's card and not a player's.

The black Charizard

Shining Charizard, Neo Destiny #107 (2002)

The first shiny Charizard in the English game, on a secret-rare slot in the last Neo set. The foil is on the dragon itself instead of the background, which is why a clean one glows in a binder page. Every modern shiny Charizard — SV49, the shiny vaults, all of it — is an echo of this card. The 1st Edition print is the serious one; the Unlimited is the version you can still find raw.

Mega era

The Mega Evolution era's Charizard. The Special Illustration Rare #125 is the art card and the biggest weight in our PF25 index; the gold #130 is the same Pokémon in the hyper-rare frame. Watch the grading gap: #130 sells for far less raw but more in a PSA 10, because the gold texture gems far less often.

The desk notes

How to collect Charizard without getting burned

Know your print before you know your price

Base Set Charizard is three different assets: 1st Edition, Shadowless and Unlimited have separate PSA labels, separate pop reports and separate price histories. The same split applies to Team Rocket and Neo Destiny. Our indices track each print as its own card, and the links on this page search for the exact print named on the row.

Modern value lives in the 10

On modern Special Illustration Rares a PSA 9 sells for about what the raw card does; the premium is almost entirely in the PSA 10. On vintage WOTC holos the gap is wider still — a PSA 10 Base Set Unlimited Charizard has sold for well over twenty times the raw mark. That is why the raw column on a vintage card is a typical raw sale in any condition, not a near-mint price.

Reprints are collectibles too — just different ones

The 2021 Celebrations and 2026 30th Celebration Classic Collection cards carry the 1999 art with a gold stamp. They are honest, affordable ways to own the artwork, and they are not the 1999 card. Any search for "Charizard 4/102" without a set name will mix all three, which is why every link here names the set.

On grading

PSA 10 is the hobby's price reference and the price column you see most here. TAG grades by machine-measured imaging with human review and publishes a full defect report for every card, which is why we link TAG slabs beside PSA on every row. We think measured grading is where the hobby is heading — that is our view, not a price claim. How TAG grading works →

Where every number came from

Method

Raw is each card's sold mark in its own ShopCardHub set index: the median of its clean ungraded sold comps (PriceCharting completed sales) over the trailing 30 days, 90 days on the 1st Edition and Shadowless indices, re-marked Monday and Thursday — the same number as the index table and the card page. PSA 9, PSA 10 and TAG 10 are read from dated completed sales whose own listing title names that grade: the median of 3+ sales in 30 days, else 2+ in 90 days. Fewer than that is no mark; a single sale is shown as one dated sale, never as a price. A raw vintage copy can be in any condition, so a raw mark on a 1999 card is a typical raw sale, not a near-mint price. Marks on this page were read between Sep 30, 2026 and Oct 5, 2026. eBay links are searches for the exact print on each row, with our affiliate tag — live listings, never a price.
