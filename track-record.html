The Scorecard · Updated Weekly

Track
Record

Every card we tell you to buy lands on this page and gets one question asked of it: was this a good card to own? We answer it at 6 months and again at 12, against real sold prices, in public. Most sites quietly delete the calls that didn't work. We keep score instead. If this page ever reads 100%, assume we started lying. And a note on the phrase "track record": some analytics sites use it for a model's signal winrate computed on marketplace asking-price aggregates. This page is the other thing — named calls with immutable projections, graded against sold prices, misses kept.

📊 How we grade ourselves. Cards aren't stocks. They're slow to sell, they move on call-ups and debuts and playoff runs that don't run on our schedule, and the people who do best in this hobby buy cards they'd be happy to hold. So we grade the way a collector actually experiences a card: would you be glad you bought it? A BUY is RIGHT if the card sells for at least 10% more than when we called it, WRONG if it sells for at least 10% less, and FLAT in between. A PASS (not at this price) is the reverse. We check at 6 months and again at 12 months, on sold prices only, and both grades stay on the page forever. The 8-week read is printed for every call so you can see what the card did early — but it is never graded. Two months is a trading clock, and nobody trades cards well on a trading clock; we tried. Nothing is ever edited after we publish a call — not the price, not the date, not the action. If we change our mind, we log a new call and grade that one too. Pokémon: we stopped calling Pokémon prices in August 2026 — we ran high on nearly every one, which means we had no edge, not bad luck. Those calls stay below, graded as they were.

A one-directional error pattern isn’t bad luck, it’s structure: a Pokémon single has one value driver (supply against the print run), and it isn’t knowable at call time. Sports cards print catalysts on a schedule; Pokémon prints rarity and nothing else. Why

Why We Do This

Because the only thing in this hobby that can't be argued with is a sold price. Our calls are opinions about where collectors will be a year from now — some will land, some won't. Publishing the ones that don't is what makes the ones that do worth anything.

How we think about cards. A card is not a stock. You can't sell it in a second, the spread is wide, and a prospect's best moment usually arrives without warning — a call-up on a Tuesday and the card is gone by Thursday. So we don't try to time it. We look for cards we'd be glad to own a year from now, buy them at a price that makes sense today, and let the player do the rest. That's the line every good collector walks between investing and collecting, and it's the only way we've found that actually works.
