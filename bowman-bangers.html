Top 5 Prospects to Chase in 2026 Bowman

The 1st Bowman card is the first officially licensed Bowman card for a prospect — issued before they earn a major-league RC designation. These are the names driving the most activity in the 2026 class. On a budget? See our best baseball cards under $50.

How to read this board. Players are ranked by what their 1st Bowman Chrome Auto actually sells for right now. Sold = a real completed sale (SportsCardsPro). Asking = what sellers are listing at on eBay tonight — always higher than sold, never a price you should pay. PSA 10 = the graded copy's sold price. ▲▼ = moved up or down since last week. Every price carries the date we read it. When a card has no real sale to point to, we say so instead of guessing. Our calls are long-hold calls. A Buy here means we think you'll be glad you own the card a year from now — not that it goes up next week. Every call is graded at 6 and 12 months on the Scorecard.

The headliner of the 2026 Bowman class and the most-chased 1st Bowman in years. Son of former MLBer Matt Holliday, Ethan is a 6-foot-4 left-handed shortstop who projects as a middle-of-the-order bat with plus raw power and bat speed. Baseball America named him the 2025 High School Player of the Year. The combination of elite draft pedigree, a famous last name, and genuine all-tools potential makes him the #1 target in this set.

MLB.com scouts describe power that is both raw and usable without over-effort — rare for a prep hitter. If Holliday develops into a middle-of-the-order threat at shortstop, his 1st Bowman Chrome Auto is the type of card that can 5–10x from current levels.
"Holliday might have as much raw and usable power as anyone from the class of '25. When at his best, the 6-foot-4 left-handed hitter can get to it without a ton of effort. He already has strength, bat speed and leverage in his swing while continuing to add physicality to his frame." — MLB.com
Verdict: Buy. A quiet week is fine after last week's move: raw eased $113 → $111 (−2%) while the PSA 10 held the $720 print it ripped to — the graded ladder re-rated and stayed there, which is what real demand looks like. Most liquid 1st Bowman in 2026. Always check current eBay sold comps before purchasing.
WHAT WOULD CHANGE OUR MIND: Bowman Chrome street date is ~Sep 9–10. If his Chrome 1st auto arrives with real sold volume and prints below the paper card, the benchmark transition goes against him and this rank is wrong. Check again ~late Sep, once Chrome has ~2–3 weeks of solds.

The most expensive 1st Bowman in the entire 2026 class — and he came off the watch tier to take it. A genuine two-way standout from South Korea who projected as a first-round KBO pick, Kim both pitches and plays shortstop and makes his U.S. debut in 2026. The two-way novelty (the Ohtani effect), the international story, and a legitimate prospect ceiling put his auto above Holliday and Fischer on the raw ladder.

Early Bowman cards of Korean two-way prospects are historically scarce, and the market has priced that scarcity fast: two straight weeks printing $131 raw — above every name that was on this board in July.
Verdict: Pass — at this price. Three straight weeks at exactly $131.25 — no seller blinking, no buyer chasing. The market found this card before we put it on the board, and $131 after a vertical run is still a price we'd rather not pay. If you already own him, hold. The value call of this class — and it already cashed. Fischer is a pure power threat at third base — he set Duke's freshman home run record and the power transferred immediately to pro ball. Scout projections of a 25-homer ceiling are conservative if his bat speed continues to optimize. "Fischer's calling card is going to be his power as he consistently gets to hard-hit contact with good bat speed, strength, and lift in his swing. His ability to launch the ball carried over into pro ball quickly, and he could be a 25-homer threat with that optimization." — MLB.com
Verdict: Hold / buy the dip — watching the $95 line. We called this a value buy at $20–$40 in June; the market printed $102, $113, $121 — and now two red weeks, $121 → $114 → $105 (−8% this week). He's above the line, so the dip call stands — but this is the week that decides which one it is. If you own him, you're still up +250% on the June call; it gets its first real grade in December on the Scorecard.

What to Skip

2026 Bowman Cards to Avoid

✗
Paper base prospects at retail price when Chrome exists — Base paper prospects are fun pack-rip fodder, but the Chrome version of the same card carries dramatically better long-term value. If you're buying to hold, buy Chrome.
✗
Ungraded parallels of players with no MLB.com ranking — Bowman has 150 prospects. Most will never reach the big leagues. Don't pay premium parallel prices for a name you found by Googling yourself.
✗
Red RC Redemption cards at inflated secondary prices — The redemption value is capped. If you're paying over $50 for a Red RC card of any player not in the top 20 prospect rankings, the math doesn't work unless he wins ROY.
✗
Pitchers beyond Gage Jump at elevated prices — Pitchers carry inherent injury risk and career trajectory variance. Gage Jump is the one arm in this class with a legitimate ceiling. Be skeptical of paying hitter-level premiums for the rest.
✗
Off-center or surface-issue Chrome autos at any price — A centering issue on a Chrome Auto is a PSA 7 waiting to happen. If you're paying $50+ for a raw auto, inspect centering carefully. A miscut Holliday auto at $70 is not a deal — it's a trap.
