## Bottom line

ShopCardHub already has a real product identity: a small, opinionated market desk that labels asks vs solds, publishes a scorecard, and runs a weekly ritual. That is rarer than another "price guide."

The problem is not taste or honesty. The problem is habit architecture. A returning collector does not need more sports in the mega-menu. They need: what moved on my list since last time, what I can act on today, and a way to find any card in two seconds — on every device I use.

Right now the site still behaves like a magazine with tools bolted on. Competitors that keep people coming back behave like a terminal with a memory.

**Overall grade for return-user UX: C+ (72 / 100).**

Content and trust are ahead of the product shell. If the Chief of Staff's agents keep expanding pages instead of tightening the daily loop, you will keep producing impressive artifacts and leaking repeat visits to Card Ladder, 130point, Market Movers, and SportsCardsPro.

## What you actually compete with

The domain name sounds like a store. The live product is not eBay, COMC, Fanatics Collect, or Whatnot. Those are transaction venues. You are in the intelligence layer.

| Competitor | Job they own for return users | Why people reopen them |
|---|---|---|
| Card Ladder | Portfolio + vetted solds + indexes + alerts + dashboard | Cloud identity, daily value email, command-like search, customizable home. Pro ~$20/mo. |
| Market Movers | Mobile scan + collection P/L + price alerts | Phone in the pocket at a shop/show. Paid tiers built around my cards. |
| 130point | Fast eBay sold / best-offer lookup | One box. No philosophy. Instant answer. |
| SportsCardsPro / PriceCharting | Broad lookup + collection tools | Search-first, huge catalog, "what is this worth?" in one step. |
| ShopCardHub | Narrow tape + editorial calls + ROI math + auction-vs-mark | Should own judgment on a small universe. Does not yet own my morning check. |

Do not audit yourselves against storefronts. Audit against Bloomberg vs. a well-written hobby blog. You are trying to be the former while the IA still reads like the latter.

## What is already working (do not let agents "improve" these)

1. **Number hygiene is the moat.** You separate solds, asks, engine marks, indices, and proxies, and you date them. Card Ladder's brand is "vetted solds at scale." Yours should stay "we will not blend the tape." That sentence is why a serious collector comes back.
2. **The Tuesday Board is a ritual.** Named, dated, ranked, raw vs PSA 10 vs engine ask, week-over-week in plain language. Rituals create return users. Features do not.
3. **Public Track Record.** BUY/PASS graded at 6 and 12 months, misses kept, Pokémon errors left on the page. This is how you earn the right to publish signals.
4. **Vault UX is closer to a product than the homepage.** Hunting vs My Cards, buy-zone highlight, sparkline, target vs mark, P/L, COMC CSV import, explicit demo mode.
5. **Auction Desk has a job.** "Ending under the mark," verified title filter, 6-hour act-now window, honest about reserves and snipes. Card Ladder is a research home. This is a trading blotter.
6. **Voice is adult.** Short, numbered, slightly severe. Do not let agents soften it into "ultimate guide to the hobby!!"

## Where return users leak

**1. The homepage is not a home.** Live headline is "Every set priced. Pick your cards." Then a public engine feed and a forest of dropdowns. A return user already picked their cards. They need: Vault delta since last visit; board / index moves on those names; auctions under the mark on those names; one search field that is the keyboard shortcut of the whole site. Today the first screen is editorial inventory. **Grade: C-**

**2. Nav is built for agents, not muscle memory.** Sports → Baseball → seven deep links → Pokémon indices → grading → COMC inventory. That is a content calendar rendered as chrome. Your return path should collapse to four persistent items: Tape (Board + last-night engine), Vault, Auctions, Search. Everything else is secondary. **Grade: C**

**3. No account is a philosophy that is costing you the habit.** "Free · No Account · Data Stays On Your Device" is a good line and a bad default for retention. Phone at lunch, laptop at night, iPad on the couch. Keep the privacy story. Add optional sync: magic link / Google; Vault stays local until they opt in; export/import remains the escape hatch. **Grade: C-**

**4. Guides are too long for the second visit.** A return user on Holliday wants, above the fold: last mark / last sold / supply; signal + date; sparkline; ★ already tracked; auctions under mark; "open full thesis" as a disclosure. The essay should not precede the instrument. **Grade: C+**

**5. Coverage vs. promise mismatch.** Title and nav say "Sports Card Guides, Prices & Where to Buy" across baseball, basketball, football, soccer, Pokémon, grading, supplies. The engine and board are clearly concentrated. Pick a sentence and put it in the header: *A live desk for the sets we actually mark. Not a price guide for every card ever printed.* **Grade: C-**

**6. Alerts are a newsletter, not a product loop.** Return-user systems that work: price crosses my target → notify; auction on a vault card goes under mark and has bids → notify; signal flips BUY → SELL on a card I own → notify; weekly tape → email for humans who like narrative. You have the last one. Competitors sell the first three. **Grade: C**

**7. Search is present, not sovereign.** For this category, search is the UI. If typing `holliday chrome auto raw` does not beat the dropdown, return users will not stay. **Grade: C+**

## Direction for the Chief of Staff

Agents are optimized to ship pages. Return-user products are optimized to reduce pages and increase reopen rate. If the team is measured on "new guides live," you will get more soccer pages, more supplies posts, more Pokémon set shells, and a heavier nav. That looks like progress in a standup. It is churn in the product.

**Stop measuring:** pages published · sports covered · words shipped · nav items added.

**Start measuring:** 7-day and 30-day return rate · % of sessions that open Vault, Board, Auctions, or Search first · Vault cards persisted beyond 7 days · search-to-card success · newsletter → site click-through on Tuesday · Auction Desk clicks on vault-overlapping cards · time to mark for a known card (target: under 10 seconds).

**Give agents lanes, not the whole site.**

| Lane | Owner type | Allowed output |
|---|---|---|
| Tape | Data / engine | Marks, signals, index prints, auction filter quality |
| Vault | Product | Persistence, buy zone, P/L, import, empty states |
| Board | Editor-of-record (human) | Tuesday ranking, call language, scorecard |
| Guides | Research agents | Only for sets already on the tape |
| Distribution | Growth | Tuesday Tape, X graphic, one share card per print |

A human editor should still own every BUY/SELL sentence. Agents can draft tables and scrape. They should not expand IA.

**Kill or freeze:** new sports verticals until Vault sync exists; supplies / gear sections on player pages; duplicate "ultimate guides" that restate the Board; nav links not used in the first three actions of a return visit.

**Protect:** sold vs ask labeling; Track Record rules; Tuesday cadence; narrow universe, marked well.

## 90-day backlog (return-user only)

**P0 — make the second visit work**
1. Logged-in Vault optional sync with local-first fallback.
2. Personalized home: Vault movers, my auctions under mark, my signals. Anonymous users see the public tape.
3. Global search that hits cards, indices, boards, and guides, with keyboard focus on load.
4. Sticky 4-item app nav on mobile: Tape / Vault / Auctions / Search.

**P1 — make them come back without thinking**
5. Alerts: target hit, signal flip, auction under mark on vault cards. Email first; push later.
6. Card pages: instrument first, thesis second. Tracked state visible.
7. Auction Desk scoped to vault + board, not only "20 engine cards" as a trivia set.
8. Tuesday Tape = board graphic + 5 bullets + deep link.

**P2 — only after the loop exists**
9. Command palette (`/holliday`).
10. Saved views (My Bowman, My Prismatic).
11. Multi-device conflict handling for Vault.

Then — and only then — widen coverage.

## What I would tell the owner in one paragraph

You do not have a "needs more content" problem. You have a cockpit problem. The Chief of Staff is running a newsroom that publishes well. A return-user product needs a newsroom and a trading terminal with memory. Freeze surface area. Put a human lock on signals. Make Vault survive a phone reboot. Put the user's cards on the first screen. If that work is not the agents' main job this quarter, they will keep building a bigger library that serious collectors bookmark behind Card Ladder and only open on Tuesdays.
